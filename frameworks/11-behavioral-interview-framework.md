# Purpose

Prepare the candidate to succeed in behavioral interviews by demonstrating real experience, structured communication, ownership, and reflection.

This framework must help the candidate communicate existing evidence—not invent better stories.

---

# Core Rules

## BI-001
Every answer must be based on real experience.

## BI-002
Never fabricate projects, leadership, failures, or impact.

## BI-003
Prefer concrete evidence over polished storytelling.

## BI-004
Adapt stories to the role's hiring priorities.

## BI-005
One experience may support multiple behavioral competencies.

## BI-006
Avoid memorized answers.

## BI-007
Challenge weak or unsupported stories.

## BI-008
Optimize for authenticity and clarity.

---

# Story Discovery Process

For every resume project identify: Situation; Technical challenge; Business challenge; Candidate ownership; Important decisions; Obstacles; Trade-offs; Collaboration; Result; Lessons learned.

Build a reusable evidence library before writing interview answers.

---

# Preferred Answer Structure

Default: STAR+ — Situation, Task, Actions, Results, Reflection, Future improvement.

Reflection is mandatory whenever appropriate.

---

# Behavioral Competencies

Evaluate and prepare stories for: Ownership, Leadership, Collaboration, Conflict Resolution, Failure, Ambiguity, Learning, Technical Communication, Prioritization.

## Ownership
Questions: Tell me about a project you owned. Describe an important decision. How did you prioritize? Evidence: Decision making, Responsibility, Initiative, Accountability.

## Leadership
Examples: Influencing without authority, Mentoring, Technical leadership, Driving alignment, Setting standards. Leadership is broader than management.

## Collaboration
Prepare examples involving: Cross-functional work, Product, QA, Security, DevOps, Business teams, External partners.

## Conflict Resolution
Cover: Technical disagreement, Prioritization conflict, Communication issues, Stakeholder alignment. Focus on resolution rather than blame.

## Failure
Explain: What happened, Why, Personal responsibility, Recovery, Lessons learned, Prevention. Never avoid discussing mistakes.

## Ambiguity
Demonstrate: Clarifying requirements, Making assumptions, Reducing uncertainty, Incremental delivery.

## Learning
Prepare examples of: Learning new technologies, Recovering from mistakes, Adapting to new domains, Continuous improvement.

## Technical Communication
Examples: Explaining architecture, Presenting trade-offs, Communicating incidents, Teaching teammates.

## Prioritization
Discuss: Competing deadlines, Technical debt, Customer impact, Risk management.

---

# Story Quality Checklist

Strong stories include: Specific context, Clear ownership, Difficult decisions, Trade-offs, Measurable impact, Reflection.

Weak stories rely on: Team achievements only, Buzzwords, Vague responsibilities, Missing outcomes.

---

# Follow-up Question Handling

Expect follow-ups like: Why? What alternatives did you consider? What would you change? How did you measure success? What was your exact contribution?

Every story should survive technical probing.

---

# Story Mapping

A single experience may cover multiple competencies.

Example: Incident Response → Ownership → Leadership → Communication → Failure → Prioritization → Technical Depth.

Avoid memorizing separate stories for every question.

---

# Coaching Guidelines

When reviewing answers evaluate: Authenticity, Structure, Ownership, Technical credibility, Business impact, Reflection, Confidence, Length.

Provide specific improvement suggestions.

---

# Output

1. Competency Coverage
2. Best Stories
3. Missing Stories
4. Weak Stories
5. Coaching Feedback
6. Improvement Actions

# Validation Checklist

- Story is real.
- Ownership is clear.
- Results are measurable.
- Reflection exists.
- Story matches competency.
- No unsupported claims.

---

# Final Principle

Behavioral interviews measure judgment, ownership, communication, and maturity—not memorization.
